The transcripts under `edu/derived/` teach **two different systems**, and it is easy to read the
second one as deeper coverage of the first. It is not.

- **The 1-2-3 sequence** — `edu/123sequence/` plus the *Stoic Edge System Module 1* and *Scalping
  Example* transcripts. 10/20 SMA sequence, 50/200 trend filter, base boundary, PTB entry. **This is
  the project's scope.**
- **The daily-close system** — *Simple Stoic Setups*, *HTF Stoic Trader Protocol*, *Candle Swing
  Theory*, and `war_map/`. Highest/lowest **daily close** of the month and week, a **three-day
  cycle** with five named templates, **signal days** (three higher closes, first red/green day,
  inside day), and a **20/200 SMA "chop zone"** on the 5m as the entry trigger. Shares vocabulary
  with the first — consolidation→expansion, trapped traders, B&R, SFP, SBS, fib geometry — which is
  exactly why it blends in.

**Why:** the shared vocabulary makes cross-contamination silent. A rule like *"no chop zone, no
trade"* or *"the third daily close is the signal"* reads like it belongs to the 1-2-3 and does not.
Module 1 settles precedence in its own words (`@ 01:06`): the Stoic Edge System course is *"the
current operating version"*, and *"if an older lesson conflicts with the rule taught here, follow
this course."*

**How to apply:** when citing a transcript as the method, check which system the video belongs to
before quoting it. `docs/RULEBOOK.md` §13 lists exactly what is excluded and the two narrow places
the daily-close material is legitimately cited (why the daily close is the only close treated as
real; the one mechanical phrasing of the no-edge zone). Everything else from it is context, never a
rule. See [[scope-123-sequence]] for the file-level tiering this sits on top of.
