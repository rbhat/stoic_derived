The transcripts under `edu/derived/` cover **two layers of the same trader's method**, and they are
easy to mistake for one because the vocabulary is shared.

- **The 1-2-3 sequence** — `edu/123sequence/` plus the *Stoic Edge System Module 1* and *Scalping
  Example* transcripts. 10/20 SMA sequence, 50/200 trend filter, base boundary, PTB entry. **This is
  the project's scope.**
- **The daily-close layer** — *Simple Stoic Setups*, *HTF Stoic Trader Protocol*, *Candle Swing
  Theory*, and `war_map/`. Highest/lowest **daily close** of the month and week, a **three-day
  cycle** with five named templates, **signal days**, and a **20/200 SMA "chop zone"** on the 5m.

**Why it matters:** these are **complementary, not rival** — `M1 @ 00:59` calls the Stoic Edge System
course *"my latest synthesis of that work"*, so the 1-2-3 is distilled **from** the daily-close
layer. Read it to understand why a rule says what it says. But `M1 @ 01:06` sets the precedence:
*"if an older lesson conflicts with the rule taught here, follow this course and the current
rulebook."*

**How to apply:** a rule from the daily-close layer may shape *whether a setup deserves risk* or
*what a realistic target is*. It never becomes a step of the sequence and never fires an entry — the
sequence and the PTB do that. Rules like *"no chop zone, no trade"* or *"the third daily close is the
signal"* read like sequence rules and are not. `docs/RULEBOOK.md` §13 holds the two constraints and
lists what has already been carried across. See [[scope-123-sequence]] for the file-level tiering
this sits on top of.
